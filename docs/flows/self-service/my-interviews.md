# My interviews

See the interview stages you have been assigned as an interviewer, and fill in or update your own scorecard for each.

## Who can use this

Every signed-in Fernly user — HR can assign anyone as an interviewer, and this screen is how you reach your assignments without needing access to the hiring board. You only ever see the interviews assigned to you.

## Where to find it

Sidebar → People → **My Interviews**.

## When to use it

- When HR assigns you as an interviewer and the interview appears in your list.
- Right after holding the interview, to record your rating and notes.
- To update a scorecard you already saved (for example to add remarks you forgot).

## Before you start

- Only interviews that are still open appear here — once a stage is decided or the candidate leaves the active pipeline, the row disappears from your list.
- The scorecard is your own; other interviewers fill theirs separately. Your remarks are shown on the stage for HR to see.
- The scheduled date and time come from HR's scheduling — if the slot is wrong, ask HR to reschedule.

## Steps

**Fill in your scorecard**
1. Find the interview row for the candidate you just met.
2. Click **Fill scorecard**.
3. Pick a **Rating (1–5)**, or leave it on "— Not rated —".
4. Choose a **Recommendation**: Strong hire, Hire or No hire (or "— None —").
5. Write what went well under **Strengths**.
6. Write what did not under **Concerns**.
7. Add **Remarks (shown on the stage)** if you want HR to see a note.
8. Click **Save scorecard**.

**Update a scorecard you already saved**
1. Find the interview row — it now shows a summary of your saved card.
2. Click **Edit scorecard**.
3. Adjust the rating, recommendation or notes.
4. Click **Save scorecard**.

## What you'll see

- The header counts what is waiting: "N assigned interviews waiting on you", or "Interviews assigned to you appear here" when none.
- The list is titled **Your assigned interviews** with the note "open stages only". Each row shows the candidate's name, the position and the interview stage label, and the scheduled time (for example "Wed 25 Feb, 10:00") — or "unscheduled" when no slot is booked.
- Once saved, a compact **Your scorecard** summary appears on the row: your rating as "N/5" and a chip with your recommendation (Strong hire, Hire, No hire, or "no recommendation").
- With nothing assigned: "No interviews assigned to you. When HR assigns you as an interviewer, the interview and its scorecard appear here."

## Common problems

**The interview you expected is not listed.**
The list shows only open stages for active candidates. If the stage was decided, skipped or the candidate moved on, the row is gone — check with HR if you are unsure.

**The time shown is "unscheduled".**
HR has not booked a slot yet. The scorecard is still available if you have already met the candidate, but normally you wait for the schedule.

**Saving fails.**
> Only the assigned interviewer or an HR/admin can fill this scorecard.

This appears if the assignment changed while you had the form open — reload the page. If the candidate is no longer active you may instead see a message that the pipeline is closed; in that case the interview is over and no scorecard is needed.

**Your remarks are visible to others.**
Yes — the field is labelled "Remarks (shown on the stage)" on purpose. Keep strengths and concerns as your structured notes and use remarks for anything you intend HR to read.

**You need to change the interview time.**
Rescheduling is done by HR from the hiring board — the time on this screen follows it automatically.

## What happens behind the scenes

- When HR schedules the interview, the slot lands on the shared calendar and a personal reminder is created for you; the candidate receives an invite email.
- Saving your scorecard records the rating, recommendation, strengths, concerns and remarks against the stage, and your summary appears on your row here.
- HR and the hiring team see your scorecard when deciding how to move the candidate forward; this screen never exposes the rest of the hiring board to you.
